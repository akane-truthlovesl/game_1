# Pass 03 · Act 3 — The Toy Room & The Studio (levels 21–30) · **v2, written after building and playing Act 2**

*Act 2 taught the thumb ten new meanings. Act 3 lets the room move and sing: **things that travel** (a snail, a train, tin toys, blocks, a cat chasing light) and **sound as logic** (order, memory, a loop you build, bass that shakes things loose). A rainy Saturday in the toy room, then the same toys at night in a recording studio, in a band.*

> Status: **designed, not built.** Written at the start of Phase 3 per the Phase Loop (`GOAL.md` §4 steps 1–2). v1 of this file was a draft written at the end of the Act 2 build; v2 (this) re-ran the creativity ritual and the distinctness test against the real build and **changed the list** (see §2). Nothing is binding until a level's card is complete (`GOAL.md` §5); the ledger rows in `GOAL.md` §6 are the contract, this is the thinking behind them.

## 1 · Debrief: what Phase 2 taught (these shape this pass)

| What the build and the bot showed | Consequence for Act 3 |
| --- | --- |
| **The most remembered levels were the sensory ones**: the dark kitchen (the plug as a lantern, the hum, a cat on a cushion), the sunrise with toast and a purring cat, the puddle drying. The weakest (once solved) were pure geometry. | Lead every Act 3 level with a *visible, audible, tactile* cause and effect. The constraint comes second. |
| **Agents multiply charm per line of code** (Biscuit cost one small state machine and gave two levels a heart). | Reuse and deepen the cat (22). New agents are tiny state machines with a face. |
| **A recipe boss works**: the order of operations (dry → feed → mend → load) was the puzzle, and the breaker taught the order by telegraphing a mistake. | Boss levels are *sequences you can read*, not searches. The act's lessons return in the order the room needs them. |
| **"You get the socket you aim at."** `engageR` (44 u) is wider than the socket pitch (40 u): a refused socket must block the release, or the plug seats next door. | Every new "refusal reason" (blocked by a block, not yet, locked) is tested for the neighbour trap by a probe with imperfect drags. |
| **Anticipation beats reflex.** A 0.8 s grace (a plug released just before a window opens waits for it) turned a timing gate into a gentle rhythm. | Act 3's timing is *anticipation with a grace*, never a reflex test. |
| **The validator's crossing model (arcs port→socket) is blind at endpoints and ignores resting cables.** The real bot count found the crossing. | Trust the bot's `cross` count; keep resting cables out of seated cables' paths. Moving things need their own crossing check at the *settled* pose. |
| **Long cables fold in S-shapes ~110 u wide**, whatever the rest point; four equal cables from collinear ports fold *in parallel* and look like a row of pendulums. | A composition rule: equal lengths, ports on one line, the same curl. Crowded rooms are fine if folds run parallel. |
| **Hand verbs are memorable** (the tape scribble). | Act 3 adds five: **draw** (21), **aim a light** (22), **flip points** (23), **twist-wind** (24), **push** (25), and later **twist-lock** (28). One verb per level, never two at once. |
| **Reach bands are unforgiving.** Nothing may sit in 0.94–1.10; two sockets 40 u apart need a ~16 % distance difference to separate by cable length. | Layouts that need forced reach put the strip or anchor *beside* the cable's root, not far below it. |
| **The meta layer is cheap and loved** (the House Map, skins, chime sets, postcards). | Each act ends with a reward: new skins/chimes; the House Map grows a floor. Act 3 earns a *lullaby chime set* and a *crayon cable* (24). |
| **The bot + a storyboard is the real gate.** It found the neighbour-seating bug, the dryer-rest crossing, and the bad dark-kitchen drag speed. | Every Act 3 mechanic gets a bot action *before* its level, and a probe script (`timer.mjs`, `dark.mjs` are the template). |

## 2 · The shape of Act 3 (v2 — the list changed, and why)

**What changed from v1:** v1 had three adjacent "autonomous strip" levels (snail, train, robot taxi) whose aha types were really one (*time*), plus three studio levels that were Simon variants. The distinctness test (`GOAL.md` §5: a level must differ from each neighbour on ≥ 3 of 9 axes; signature object, aha type and surprise unique within the act) fails on 21/22/23 and 27/29. **v2:** one *carried strip* engine, but each use gets a different hand verb and a different feeling (draw it a road, route it with points, wind it up); **Taxi!** (a robot vacuum you summon by tapping) moves to Act 4's Server Hall, where a maintenance trolley is the right home; **the cat returns** as a mechanic (herd her with a spot of light) in the slot v1 gave to a drifting sunbeam; **Block Party** (push toy blocks, a Sokoban blend) is new and closes the toy room; the studio half is built on **one new signature object, the step-sequencer strip** (each socket is a beat; the loop plays what you build), so 27, 29 and 30 share an instrument but not an aha.

**Distinctness check (v2, nine axes: silhouette · palette/light · signature object · sound voice · mechanic combination · aha type · pace · emotional beat · cast).** Neighbours 21–22: object, aha type, pace, beat, cast, sound differ (6/9). 22–23: object, aha, palette, sound, pace, cast (6/9). 23–24: object, aha, sound, palette, beat (5/9). 24–25: object, aha, pace, palette, sound (5/9). 25–26: world, object, aha, palette, sound, beat (6/9). 26–27: object, aha, sound, pace, beat (5/9). 27–28: object, aha, sound, pace, beat, palette (6/9). 28–29: object, aha, sound, pace, beat (5/9). 29–30: scale (boss), beat, aha, cast (4/9; the boss composes, so it shares material by design). Aha types are unique inside the act: *draw, herd, route, wind, push, read, rebuild, lock, accent, conduct*.

**Time of day / mood / music root:** 21–25 the long grey Saturday afternoon, the sun breaking through at 22 and low and golden by 25; 26–30 night. `Audio.setRoot` Act 3 = +3 (a third above Act 2).

**New engine, and what it reuses (each new object is a goal-less toy room first):**
1. **The carried strip** (`strip.carrier`): a strip whose position is a function of time or of the player's hand, along a path (drawn, fixed, or walked). It reuses the rail machinery (`reachAt` as samples, `railSpanOK`, the soft taut-cable wall), adds a clock, a pause, and a *strain pop* (a plug the cable can no longer follow is gently pulled out: a *pop*, never a failure). 21, 23, 24.
2. **The herding agent** (`mech-cat.js`, grown from `mech-dark.js`): a chase target (the light spot), pounce / overshoot / lose-interest, sit on a socket. 22.
3. **Pushables** (`def.blocks`): blocks on a grid that slide when dragged (clack, friction), hide sockets, are obstacles for the geodesic (`Geom.invalidate` on every move; reach tables cached by block layout). 25.
4. **The twist** (a recogniser like the tape scribble: tangential travel around a point): winds tin toys (24) and locks plugs (28).
5. **The sequencer strip** (`strip.seq = {bpm, steps}`): each socket is a step; a running light walks the strip; a seated plug triggers its device's voice at its step, pitch from the socket (pentatonic). Rules know the *target* (a list of device-at-step) or the *accent map*. 27, 29, 30.
6. **Dependencies** (`device.after`): a device stays in standby until its prerequisite is awake (the Level 15 vocabulary, between devices instead of strips). 26.
7. **Shakes** (`def.shake`: periodic bass impulses): unlocked plugs jump out of their sockets on the beat; locked plugs hold. 28.

Level 28 · "Bass Drop"                   Act 3 · the studio · a speaker the size of a wall · club tremor   (as built)
Aha:          Every thump of the bass shakes unlocked plugs out of their sockets. A quarter-turn twist locks a plug; it then needs a twist to come out.
Signature:    The twist-lock plug (a ring around the plug that clicks), and the speaker wall.
Soul + cast:  Tremor and mischief; a joke where the room tries to throw your work back. Cast: Wall (the huge speaker), Lefty and Righty, Boxy, Spike.
Form:         The stage floor; the speaker wall at the top; the strip in front; ripples run across the floor with every beat; the devices wobble in time.
Thumb's journey: Plug a device: on the next kick it hops out with a *pop* and flies home (never a failure: it just goes home). Plug it and **twist** (a quarter-turn around the plug): a click, a collar locks, and the next thump only shivers it. Lock them all and the bass goes round the room as ripples.
The trap:     Plugging all four fast before the next kick; they all pop (it teaches the lock). Or twisting one *before* seating it (nothing to lock): the collar spins free. Teaches: seat, then twist.
Delight:      The bass ripples travel across the floor to the cables (they shimmy); the locked collar glows; **wow:** when the last one locks the bass drops out and the room breathes.
Wake-up gag:  The speaker wall blushes and plays a gentle chord instead.
Sound twist:  A sub-bass thump (tuned, 1 per beat), the lock's brass click.
Secret / caption / teaser:  Hold a plug during a thump and let it go on the beat: a gold plug. "Lock it in." Teaser: pads flashing in time.
Validator: cls teach · solutions: static (the lock is a gesture; every arrangement is legal) · p_luck high by design · par 4 · Bot: twists with real circular drags.

- **Weight and texture (the feel):** a held block follows the thumb with a **spring that has a speed limit and an acceleration limit** (`FEEL`: 17/s, 560 u/s, 4400 u/s²; Blocky 11/s, 400 u/s, 2600 u/s²), so a flick lags and then catches up; it lifts (scale, tilt with its velocity, a longer shadow), **stops against and slides along** other blocks and the box, and when you let go between cells it **settles with a little overshoot** and a hollow wooden thunk. **Every block is a marimba bar**: it plays its letter's note when it bumps something (and the block it hit shivers and plays its own): the box can be played; below a gentle push a bump is silent. A scrape of wood on felt follows how fast a block moves. **Blocky** (2x2, heavy) has a face: eyes that follow the drag, brows, a round mouth when he bumps, a smile at the Wake. Low golden sun: every block throws a long fading shadow to the lower right.
- **The level:** a box of 7 x 3 cells holding Blocky and nine letter blocks over a power strip lying on its floor: one outlet in the open (amber), two under Blocky; the three devices above with their plugs resting on the felt. The goal is **cells, not geometry**: the three outlets and the cell above each must be free (the way the plug comes in). The shortest way out is **seven moves** (breadth-first search over bitmasks: blocks of one size are interchangeable; ~52 000 layouts, 0.2 s); 400 sampled goal layouts all keep every cable in reach (worst ratio 0.90). `par` is 3: block moves are free, only re-plugging costs the "clean" star.
- **The Wake:** S, N, U, G hop out of the box to the top of the mat, each landing on its note with its letter turned gold, and spell **SNUG** over the awake room; Biscuit naps beside them; the devices clap; far away a needle comes down on a record (**the teaser** of Level 26). **The secret:** S, N, U, G side by side in a row, in order, *anywhere, even for a moment*: the letters glow gold and a golden block appears.
- **What the build taught:** (1) a **single block barely lengthens a long cable's route** (a 44 u block on the line adds ~1 %), so blocks only work as obstacles when they form a *wall*; and a puzzle whose goal is "the cable reaches" needs a search over exact geodesics per layout (10 ms each): far too slow. Making the goal **a set of free cells** turned it into a bitmask search that runs in 0.2 s, in the browser too, and the exact reach is only checked on the layouts that matter; (2) a `Float32Array` for Dijkstra distances made valid heap entries look stale (a rounded-down stored value is smaller than the double popped): half the field silently went missing: use `Float64Array` (the fine-grid reach code was removed once the cell goal replaced it, but the lesson stays); (3) the first layout (devices below, strip above, a long cable) gave 380 u cables resting 140 u from their ports and loops off the mat: **the vertical budget decides the cable lengths**: put the outlets close to the devices; (4) with identical blocks and free-drag moves, open space makes any puzzle trivial: tightness comes from the box (a closed tray), and the pocket for the biggest block must exist in the solved layout (a 7-cell-wide tray with outlets at columns 1, 3, 5 leaves no 2 x 2 pocket and is unsolvable).
- **Verified here:** `test-rules` 155, validator ok (`verifyBlocks`), real-drag bot 10 actions (7 block drags + 3 plugs) / 3 stars / no crossings / 60 fps, `tools/playtest/blocks.mjs` (lift and weight, the lag, whole-cell settling, collisions never overlap, the knock plays marimba notes, a block cannot leave the box, undo, the plan with real drags, the ta-da, plugs seat, a plug is solid, the tether sends a block back, the secret), `hints.mjs`. **Owed to a phone:** whether the numbers in `FEEL` are patient or sluggish, the scrape's level, the marimba notes against the chord pad, whether the outlets under Blocky are discovered without the hint, and the two-thumb case (a block with one thumb, a plug with the other).

**C6 Level 26 "Order Up" (built, 2026-11-14).**
- **The mechanic (`Rules.chainState/chainOK/chainDepth`, `src/mech-chain.js`):** devices can **listen** to one another (`device.after`: the id of the device they follow) and **feud** (`device.clash`). *The beat runs left to right along a strip*: a listener is awake only while the one it follows is awake **somewhere to its left on the same strip**; two feuding devices in **neighbouring sockets** howl with feedback and both go quiet. The wrong side, another strip, a sleeping leader, a neighbour: each is a different *reason* a device waits, and `Rules.chainState` returns it (`why`), so the bubble over a waiting device says exactly what is missing. The solver filters complete arrangements with `Rules.chainOK`; `Rules.power` demotes listeners to *standby* (the state the engine already had), so completion, the Wake, luck and the validator all follow without special cases (luck now treats a chain level like a fed one).
- **The world (`src/art-studio.js`, a new `studio` world):** a dark plywood stage with gaffer tape and coloured spike marks under a black rubber mat with a hand-chalked border and one warm pool of light; the **flight case** with its charcoal egg-crate foam and aluminium trim round the strip; **Tick the metronome** (a tapered wooden case with a face, a brass pivot, a pendulum with a sliding weight that swings in time while he is awake); the **roadie's chalkboard** (the signal chain drawn from the level's data: circles with each device's colour, chalk arrows, a bolt for a feud; an arrow glows warm when its follower is awake; three taps smudge it and a golden guitar pick appears); a masking-tape **name** under each device.
- **Feel and feedback:** a soft *tock* at each end of Tick's swing while the room is still being put together; a **light runs along the strip to the right** from every awake leader and rings each awake listener's socket as it passes; listeners wake **in chain order, each with a relay click** as the beat reaches it (0.3 s per link); a waiting device wears a **bubble** with an arrow and the leader's name (or a bolt and the feuding neighbour's), blinking; two neighbours **screech** (a sawtooth sweep), squint, and say *eeeee!*; the Wake begins with **Tick counting the band in** on a woodblock, "one, two, three, four", each with a hop and a caption, and only then do the devices wake; far away, four sockets hum (the teaser of Level 27).
- **The level:** five devices in a row (Tick, Pip, Lefty, Righty, Lumi) over one six-socket strip; chalk: Tick -> Pip -> {Lefty, Righty}, Lefty feuds with Righty, Lumi listens to nobody. **Every cable reaches every socket** (no ambiguous reach: ratios 0.65-0.92), so the freedom is the order: 20 arrangements work, **exactly one is crossing-free** (Tick, Pip, Lefty, a gap, Righty, Lumi), p_luck 0.03. Plugging under the devices from left to right is the natural first try and is wrong by one socket: the spare socket is a buffer.
- **What the build taught:** (1) *tidy and order are the same constraint*: cables cross exactly when the slot order differs from the devices' x order, so with a Tidy star in the level the leader-left rule is automatically consistent with the row, and the rule is learned by experiment (a bubble tells you) rather than by being forced; (2) reach limits and "no ambiguous reach" cannot both hold on a 44 u socket pitch with ~300 u cables (the ratio moves 6-8 % per socket, the band is 16 %), so a level about order must make every cable reach every socket, and long cables need their resting plugs *far* from their ports and the curls chosen so the arcs stay on the mat (a quick scan of `Rope.restPoints` found the positions); (3) `Art.decor` was handed only a few fields of a decor's runtime, so custom fields (the block wall's wobble, the board's lit arrows) never reached the art: it now gets the whole runtime object (this also fixes the wobble of Level 24's blocks, which the probe could not see because it read the field, not the picture).
- **Verified here:** `test-rules` 157, validator ok, real-drag bot 5 actions / 3 stars / no crossings, `tools/playtest/chain.mjs` (14 checks: a follower alone waits with a bubble that names its leader, the leader on its right does not wake it, moved left it does with a relay click and the arrow lights, neighbours screech and go quiet, a gap cures it, the Wake counts four woodblock hits, the secret). **Owed to a phone:** whether the rule is discovered from the bubbles and the board without the hint, whether the screech is funny or painful through real speakers, the count-in's tempo, and whether five long cables are a charming tangle or a mess.

**C7 Levels 27 and 29 (built, 2026-11-16): the sequencer strip (`src/mech-seq.js`).**
- **The engine:** a strip whose sockets are the steps of a loop. A light walks along them one step per beat; a device has `seq {step, voice, note, vel}`. On a wrong step a plain sequencer (L27) sends the device to standby and the loop buzzes at it; a **groove** (`strip.seq.groove`, L29) keeps the voice sounding, ragged, and never sits it out. The room plays the whole tune once at the start (the demo), and again on a tap on the strip or on the hint. Notes are scheduled 0.14 s ahead on the audio clock. Until the first gesture there is no audio clock, so the loop runs on the room clock and re-anchors when audio wakes. A tap on a device plays its voice.
- **Rules:** `Rules.seqOK` (the solver: exact pockets), `seqWrong`, `power` (standby, L27 only), `grooveScore` / `grooveLit` (inside `Rules.complete`), `L.seqGroove`. The solver is untouched for L27.
- **World and sound:** the studio's chalk staff (it writes each note as the loop plays it), step lamps under the sockets, the flight case. Voices: `wood`, `pluck`, `bell`, `celesta` (L27); `kick`, `snare`, `tom`, `hat`, `chord`, `bass`, `bell` (L29, all in `A.inst`). Teasers: `thumpFar` (L27, for 28) and `spotOn` (L29, for 30).
- **Two secrets, both about hearing:** L27, the tune rebuilt with no wrong plug at any moment (a music-box key). L29, a tap on the silent pad *on its beat* ("That's the pocket.", a golden drumstick).
- **What the build taught:** (1) a silent step must still push an event, or the light stops on it and the demo loses its rest; (2) the audio clock is absent before the first gesture, so the probes click once and the loop re-anchors cleanly; (3) a per-frame gradient over a big rectangle cost about 8 fps on L29 (its glow now draws only while the downbeat swells); the seven long cables (2229 u, the most of any level) are the rest of the cost, and they run at about 55 fps headless; (4) a tidy groove is a geometry fact: the devices must stand in the groove's order (so the trap is one pad early, not a tangle) and the bulge sign must keep seven arcs apart (`settleCurl −0.25`); (5) reach for eight pads at the 38 u minimum pitch needs every cable to reach every pad at ratio ≤ 0.9, or the ambiguous band fails; that makes long cables and rests far enough from their ports to avoid a heap; (6) eight pads at pitch 38 overhang a 324 u mat by 14 u on the standard body: a per-strip `pad` (4 u) fits it; (7) the bot's level loop assumed contiguous ids and silently skipped Level 29: it now walks the real list.

**C8 Level 28 "Bass Drop" (built, 2026-11-16): the kick clock, the pops, the twist-lock (`src/mech-bass.js`).**
- **The engine:** the clock keeps the kicks on the audio clock (the same lookahead and room-clock fallback as the sequencer). A kick pops every seated plug that is not locked and past its grace, shivers the rest, and sends a ring out over the floor. The collar (`st.flags.locked`, a per-plug collar runtime) is the only new state: `Rules.power` stands a seated plug by until it is locked (only when the live room has lock flags, so the solver and the validator see every arrangement as legal, and Rules.complete needs every collar closed). `Rules.hint` returns `lock` for a plug that sits in its place and is not locked, drawn as a hand walking a quarter turn round the collar.
- **The gesture:** a press on a seated plug is a *grip*. Inside 46 u of the plug, the finger's travel round the plug's centre is summed (`bassGrip`); past a quarter turn (1.25 rad) it toggles the lock. Outside 46 u, an unlocked plug is pulled the ordinary way and a locked plug refuses ("twist it"). The grip is checked in the room's seated-grab block, so the rest of the grab code is untouched.
- **What the build taught:** (1) the pop must use `hopTo` (it clears the seat and runs the hop); the draft's hand-release of the rope fought it; (2) a grip starts only when the plug is `seated` (not while it slides home), so the bot and the probe wait for that state; (3) the bot's post-seat pause (0.9 s) is longer than the grace: on bass levels the seat is followed by a 150 ms pause, and the twist is a separate action; (4) Chrome logs `navigator.vibrate` refusals before the first touch, which the hints audit counts as an error: haptics now wait for `navigator.userActivation` (a one-line guard that also quiets the other levels); (5) the lock hint walks the same arc the twist does, so the bulb teaches the gesture, not just the plug.
- **Layout:** four devices at 56, 140, 232, 306 u with cables all reaching all four sockets at ratio ≤ 0.9 (lengths 185–268), rests in the clear band above the devices at a ratio of about 1.1–1.2 (a rest 35 u from its port made a heap, and rests at 300 coiled across the neighbours; moving them up made the arcs read). Tidy is the monotone order; `settleCurl −0.25`.
- **Verified here:** `test-rules` 176 (the lock standby, the solver's 24 legal arrangements, the lock hint), validator ok (24 arrangements, one tidy, margin 54 %, luck waived with the reason), real-drag bot 8 actions (4 seats, 4 twists) / 3 stars / par / no crossings / 60 fps (`bot.mjs 28`), `tools/playtest/bass.mjs` (12 checks: the 0.652 s kick on the audio clock, a plug is safe through its grace and popped by the first kick after it, a twist within the grace locks it, it stays through two kicks, a pull on it is refused, a twist back frees it and a pull takes it, the secret held through a kick and seated on the beat, the wake), `hints.mjs` 28 (the bulb shows the seat hint; the lock hint is exercised by the bot and the storyboard, `shots.mjs 28`).
