# 06 · The Idea Garden

~120 mechanic seeds. **This is a springboard, not a checklist and not a ceiling.** Every seed is written to be *misused*: bent, crossed with another seed, pushed until it becomes something else. If a seed makes you think of a better one, write the better one in the margin and keep going.

**How to read an entry:** **Name**: what you see and do. *Aha:* the insight it can give. *Spring:* where it could go next. `[cost · wow]` where cost is **S** (hours), **M** (a day or two), **L** (a week-ish) and wow is ★ to ★★★ (how much delight/novelty per unit of cost).

**How to pick:** a level uses **at most three active mechanics**, **at most one of them new** (chapter 09). Prefer seeds that (a) create a *visible* rule, (b) have a physical, tactile verb, and (c) give a one-sentence aha. Prefer seeds that *combine* (see the mashup matrix at the end) over seeds that stand alone.

## A · Keys and compatibility (what fits what)

1. **Shape-and-color keys**: plugs and sockets carry a glyph and color; they must match. *Aha:* trivial alone, rich in combination. *Spring:* seasonal key sets; keys as a language. `[S · ★]` **(in Act 1)**
2. **Joker sockets**: a universal socket takes anything. *Aha:* save the joker for the plug with no other home. *Spring:* a joker that locks to the first plug that ever uses it; two jokers where *order* matters. `[S · ★★]` **(Act 1)**
3. **Chameleon plugs**: a plug cycles to the next key every time it is seated or pulled; plan the sequence so each socket sees its color. *Spring:* the cycle shown as a tiny color wheel on its back; cycle-on-shake. `[M · ★★]`
4. **Two-faced plugs**: reversible, like USB-C. Tap a held or resting plug to flip it between two keys with a satisfying flick. *Spring:* a plug with *three* faces; faces that show different glyphs on each side. `[S · ★★]`
5. **Adapter dongles**: loose objects. Drop one into a socket to change the socket's key. Dongles take a slot and can chain. *Aha:* the shortest key-conversion path. *Spring:* fat dongles that cover neighbors; a dongle you must carry through an obstacle. `[M · ★★★]`
6. **Voltage and magic smoke**: 5 V / 12 V / 230 V keys; a mismatch coughs a harmless puff and ejects the plug, leaving a permanent soot smudge on the mat. *Spring:* a room that accumulates soot smudges like a diary of mistakes (the postcard shows them). `[S · ★★]`
7. **Picky neighbors**: sockets (or plugs) *dislike* certain neighbors; a tiny frown appears between incompatible adjacent plugs. *Aha:* it is a seating chart. *Spring:* "The Wedding Strip" with a tolerance for exactly one feud. `[M · ★★]`
8. **Taped keys**: plug keys are hidden under tape until you tap (peel) them; peeking is free but you must remember. *Spring:* peel by shaking; the tape grows back after 10 s. `[S · ★★]`
9. **Lock-and-key**: a socket sits behind a padlock that opens when a *specific* device is awake (power the doorbell to open the gate). The first dependency chain. `[M · ★★]`
10. **Polarity**: a plug has a visible notch; tap to flip 180° until it fits the socket's notch. The micro-gesture teaches *tap-on-plug*. `[S · ★]`
11. **Multi-key sockets**: a socket that accepts *two* keys (coral or mint). Compatibility becomes a bipartite graph with interesting structure. *Aha:* Hall's marriage theorem in a desk. `[S · ★★]`
12. **Key wheel**: a rotating knob on the strip sets which key it accepts *right now*; turning it changes all its sockets at once (a two-state strip you configure). `[M · ★★]`

## B · The cable itself

13. **Reach**: fixed length measured along the path. *Aha:* the shortest cable goes first. *Spring:* reach as the central currency across the game. `[S · ★★]` **(Act 1)**
14. **Stretchy cable**: a gum cable stretches to 1.6× and pulls back hard. *Aha:* the stretched plug **flings** when released; use it to sling across a gap. *Spring:* slingshot bank shots; a rubber cable that *snaps back and unplugs others*. `[M · ★★★]`
15. **Coil cord**: a telephone-style coil with huge reach that boings and wobbles for a second after any move. *Spring:* a coil that tangles with its own twin. `[S · ★★]`
16. **Pegs and posts**: cables wrap around pegs, shortening effective reach but letting the plug turn corners. *Aha:* string-art routing. *Spring:* a forest of pegs ("Threading the Needle"); pegs that slide. `[M · ★★★]`
17. **Cable clips**: drag a cable body into a clip: it snaps and holds that segment (and its bend radius). Clipped cables cannot cross. *Spring:* lanes; a clip that releases when a device wakes. `[M · ★★]`
18. **Bridges (cable covers)**: crossing is allowed only on a rubber ramp. *Aha:* planarity with a limited number of bridges. `[M · ★★]`
19. **Thick cables**: hose-fat cables that do not fit through narrow gaps. *Spring:* a "bully cable" that physically pushes thin cables aside. `[M · ★★]`
20. **Frayed cable**: spits sparks if moved fast or bent tightly; move slowly (steady hands) or the plug pops out of your grip. *Spring:* a frayed cable you can *only* use if you tape it first. `[S · ★★]`
21. **Electrical tape**: hold-press a frayed section to wrap it, a tactile repair verb. *Spring:* limited tape rolls; tape also fixes a cracked socket. `[S · ★★]`
22. **Spliced cable**: a cable with a connector midway; disconnect it and the far half becomes a loose extension. *Spring:* modular cable building ("Lego cables"). `[L · ★★★]`
23. **Retractable reel**: hold to extend, release to retract slowly. The cable is always taut. *Aha:* beat the retraction, or latch by plugging. *Spring:* a tape-measure level where the plug fights the spring. `[M · ★★]`
24. **Y-splitter**: the plug end branches to two devices; the branch knot is draggable; both branches need to reach the knot. A mini tree puzzle. `[M · ★★]`
25. **String of lights**: a cable with eight bulbs along its body (series). One dud bulb breaks the chain; tap bulbs to swap with a spare. *Spring:* a whole holiday world. `[M · ★★★]`
26. **Loose two-ended cables**: an extension cord with a plug at *each* end; place both. Creates real power networks. `[M · ★★★]`
27. **Knotted cable**: a granny knot you untie by pulling the plug through the loop in a circular drag. Authored loop state, not true rope topology. `[L · ★★★]`
28. **Magnetic plug ends**: snaps from afar and sticks to *any* metal (paperclips, rails); decoy snaps; a satisfying *peel*. `[S · ★★]`
29. **Glow cables in the dark**: neon cables light the unlit room; the room reveals itself as cables wake things. Mostly beauty; a mood for a whole level set. `[S · ★★★]`
30. **Heavy armored cable**: drags lighter cables when moved (cable-cable mass interaction). `[M · ★]`

## C · Strips, sockets and machines

31. **2/3/4/6-socket strips**, vertical and diagonal, different pitches. *Spring:* pitches that vary along a strip. `[S · ★]` **(Act 1)**
32. **Switches**: plug first, flip after, and the whole strip cascades. *Aha:* delayed gratification. *Spring:* several switches wired to subsets; a **master** switch that must be last. `[S · ★★★]` **(Act 1, Level 5)**
33. **Dead sockets**: sooty, spark and refuse. *Spring:* revive one with a brush tool; a dead socket that *hides* (looks fine until tried). `[S · ★]` **(Act 1)**
34. **Fat bricks**: footprint covers neighbors; the classic power-brick-on-a-strip problem. *Aha:* bricks go at the ends. *Spring:* 2-D strips (a socket grid) with bricks that cover four. `[S · ★★★]` **(Act 1)**
35. **Elbow plugs**: a right-angle plug overhangs only on the side its cable comes from. *Aha:* approach direction chooses orientation. `[M · ★★]`
36. **Sliding strips on rails**: drag a strip along a groove; Rush Hour for power. *Spring:* two strips fighting over one rail; a strip that clicks into detents. `[M · ★★★]`
37. **Lazy Susan strips**: a circular strip you spin by dragging its rim; cables **wind** around the hub (shortening) and unwind when you spin back. `[L · ★★★]`
38. **Strips with their own plug**: they must be plugged in to energize; a second layer of puzzle (daisy chains). *Spring:* a chain of three strips where the first has the only wall access. `[M · ★★★]`
39. **Wattage and breakers**: each device draws N; strips have capacity; overload trips with a clack and sparks; flip the switch to reset. A glowing ring shows load. `[M · ★★]`
40. **Timer sockets**: live in a visible pulse (2 s on, 3 s off). Seat during the window. *Spring:* phased timers (a metronome ring) where the player must plug in rhythm. `[M · ★★]`
41. **Hidden sockets**: under a book or in the dark; reveal by moving the object or lighting it. `[M · ★★]`
42. **Shutters**: a child-safety shutter you slide with one finger while the other holds the plug: the first two-thumb moment. `[M · ★★]`
43. **Shuffling sockets**: after every seat the strip's sockets shuffle on a carousel; plan for a *forecast*. `[M · ★★]`
44. **Moving sockets**: on a conveyor, a toy-train wagon, a Ferris wheel gondola, a Roomba's back. Timing plus reach versus motion. `[L · ★★★]`
45. **Mirrored pairs**: seating one socket also lights its twin; one plug wakes two devices. *Spring:* symmetrical rooms. `[M · ★★]`
46. **Fused sockets**: each socket pops after one use. Permanent. Resource puzzles; fuse replacements are collectibles. `[S · ★★]`
47. **Hungry sockets**: a suction socket that pulls plugs within 80 u; approach from the shielded side. `[M · ★★]`
48. **Pop-up grommets**: round desk sockets that rise when tapped and sink when idle (a tactile reveal). `[S · ★★]`
49. **Strip with a pitch ruler**: sockets spaced at *irregular* pitches so a fat brick fits in one gap and not another; spatial detective work. `[S · ★★]`
50. **Wall plates behind furniture**: a socket behind a drawer: open the drawer (drag) to reach it. `[M · ★★]`

## D · Devices that *do* things (the chain-reaction layer)

*Powering one device changes the room for another. The order of waking is the puzzle.*

51. **Fan**: blows a cone of wind that moves cables and plugs. *Aha:* straighten a snagged cable, or *blow a plug across a gap*. *Spring:* oscillating fans; fan + steam. `[M · ★★★]`
52. **Lamp**: lights a cone in a dark room; drag its head to aim. Reveals hidden sockets and keys. `[M · ★★★]`
53. **Heater / hair dryer**: melts ice sockets and dries puddles (wet sockets zap). *Spring:* dry the right puddle first. `[M · ★★]`
54. **Electromagnet crane**: when powered, lifts a metal obstacle (toolbox, paperclip) and drops it elsewhere along a rail. *Aha:* powering a device moves a blocker. `[L · ★★★]`
55. **Speaker with bass**: thumps shake the desk; loose items jitter; *loose* plugs unseat. *Spring:* use the bass to shake a stuck item free; or turn it off first. `[M · ★★]`
56. **Robot vacuum**: patrols, drags cables, docks, and can be a *taxi* carrying a strip. `[L · ★★★]`
57. **Toy train**: circles a track carrying a socket wagon; power starts it; seat as it passes, or flip a signal to stop it. `[L · ★★★]`
58. **Conveyor belt**: moves everything on it; a "sushi belt" of plugs to catch. `[M · ★★★]`
59. **Projector**: casts shadows that hide things; the shadow of a moving obstacle sweeps the wall. `[M · ★★]`
60. **Garage door / blinds / elevator**: opens a path or lifts a platform when powered; reveals the next area. `[M · ★★]`
61. **Metronome / clock**: when powered it ticks all timers in the room; speed is a lever. `[S · ★★]`
62. **Drone**: when awake it carries a plug on a tether to where you steer a target; battery limits flight. *"Drone delivery."* `[L · ★★★]`
63. **Tesla coil**: arcs jump to metal within 70 u. Arcs can *wirelessly power* a device in range (an alternative to cable) but zap cables in range. `[L · ★★★]`
64. **Solar panel and blinds**: powers only under a sunlight beam that moves with the day; slide the blinds to aim it. `[M · ★★]`
65. **Power bank**: a portable source; carry it near a distant device and plug in; drains slowly. `[M · ★★]`
66. **Hand-crank generator**: tap rhythmically to keep it running while you plug with the other thumb. `[M · ★★]`
67. **Hamster wheel**: a living generator that runs while the *speaker's music* plays. Speaker → hamster → lamp, a three-step chain with a tiny story. `[M · ★★★]`
68. **Doorbell**: powering it summons an agent (a delivery bot or the cat). `[M · ★★]`
69. **Humidifier / mist**: a cloud that hides part of the room for a while. `[S · ★]`
70. **Disco ball**: when awake, lights sweep the room and *reveal* hidden keys on the beat. `[S · ★★★]`

## E · Living rooms (agents with their own agenda)

71. **The Cat (Biscuit)**: sits on warm things (awake devices), bats dangling plugs, unplugs strips to nap on. *Aha:* lure it by dangling a plug; it sits on a socket until you lure it off; pet it to make it stay. See chapter 07. `[L · ★★★]`
72. **Robot vacuum**: (also D56) chases the cable tail and eats slack. `[L · ★★★]`
73. **Toddler wave**: every N seconds a toddler toddles to the strip and pulls a plug; distract with a toy. A slapstick rhythm; plugs simply pop out. `[M · ★★]`
74. **Goose**: honks and steals your *held plug* if you pass too near; chase it down. `[M · ★★★]`
75. **Mouse**: chews frayed cables (damage spreads); tape or swap. `[M · ★★]`
76. **Ghost**: flickers lights and unplugs in the dark; a powered lamp repels it. Ghost cables replay *your own previous attempt* (see H1). `[L · ★★★]`
77. **Roommate ghost cursor**: an AI "second player" plugging things in simultaneously; leave it room. `[M · ★★]`
78. **Wind-up toys**: wind (twist) and aim them; they walk toward sockets and *carry plugs* to far places. `[M · ★★★]`
79. **Birds on the wire**: land on cables (sag, pitch) and flutter off if you drag fast. Steady hands outdoors. `[M · ★★]`
80. **Growing plants**: grow toward light and overgrow sockets; trim (tap to snip) or power the grow-light to steer. `[M · ★★]`
81. **Snail mail**: a snail with a socket on its shell crosses the desk slowly. The slowest moving socket in the game. `[S · ★★★]`
82. **The Plug Gremlin**: a tiny creature that slips *extra plugs* into your strip; find and evict it. Hidden-object flavor. `[M · ★★]`
83. **Night-shift robots**: little bots patrol and share sockets; cooperative scheduling. `[L · ★★]`
84. **The Baby (asleep)**: a sleeping baby must *not* be woken: a loud device nearby wakes it; quiet puzzle in reverse. `[M · ★★]`

## F · Space, physics and rule-bending worlds

85. **Zero-g**: no friction, plugs drift, cables swirl; flick and let go; magnet captures at speed; bank shots off walls. *A completely different feel.* `[L · ★★★]`
86. **Wall mode (gravity)**: side-on; cables sag; swing and release at the apex into a socket. *Spring:* pendulum timing. `[L · ★★★]`
87. **Ice floor**: plugs slide; cables slide; use walls to stop. `[M · ★★]`
88. **Underwater**: buoyant cables float up; viscous drag; bubbles; sealed socket caps; fish nibble. `[L · ★★★]`
89. **Wind tunnel**: constant wind with gusts; slack bows; time your drops between gusts. `[M · ★★]`
90. **Magnetic floor**: plugs deflect; N/S polarity visuals; attract-repel puzzles. `[M · ★★]`
91. **Tilt table**: the phone's accelerometer sets gravity on the desk; slack slides downhill; on-screen arrows as fallback. `[M · ★★★]`
92. **Rotating room**: drag the room's rim to rotate it by 90°; with gravity, cables fall differently. Monument Valley feel. `[L · ★★★]`
93. **Two-layer desk**: the desk and the floor below; plugs dropped over the edge dangle; vertical distance costs length. *"Under the Desk."* `[L · ★★★]`
94. **Trapdoors and holes**: plugs dropped in a hole fall to the layer below. `[M · ★★]`
95. **Dollhouse (scale shift)**: zoom into a dollhouse on the desk with its own tiny strip and devices; solving the miniature powers a device in the real room. Recursion. `[L · ★★★]`
96. **Sliding desk sections**: parts of the surface slide on tracks; devices ride them. `[M · ★★]`
97. **Honey and soap**: zones where plugs stick or slide; cables stretch in honey. `[S · ★★]`
98. **Low-light torch**: the plug *carries a small light* that reveals sockets as you pass. `[M · ★★★]`
99. **Gravity lever**: flips gravity up/down; cables fall to the ceiling; plugs hang. `[M · ★★]`

## G · Win conditions and puzzle structures

100. **Power all**: the base.
101. **Power exactly**: some devices must stay asleep (baby monitor, alarm clock): selective waking. `[S · ★★]`
102. **Order**: boot sequence; wake A before B or B shuts down; visible dependency arrows. `[M · ★★]`
103. **Melody (Simon)**: devices are notes; reproduce the shown melody by plugging in order; wrong notes unplug with a sour *plink*. `[M · ★★★]`
104. **Lit-Brite**: devices are pixels in a grid; the lit set must form a picture; nonogram-style numbers on the sides. `[M · ★★★]`
105. **Budget**: a limited number of seat actions; a ring counts them. Optional challenge stars. `[S · ★]`
106. **Chain through**: devices pass power on (reverse-charging phones); the *last* device must be reached via others. `[M · ★★]`
107. **Race (soft)**: a rising tide or setting sun. Missing it just switches to "night mode" with extra glow. `[M · ★★]`
108. **Survive**: keep the room awake for 10 s while an agent attacks. `[M · ★★]`
109. **Reverse (unplug)**: start plugged and overloaded; unplug in the right order to avoid a trip. Jenga for power. `[M · ★★★]`
110. **Escape-room desk**: powering objects reveals clues (a safe's code from device screens). Tiny story beats. `[L · ★★★]`
111. **Zen / sandbox**: no goal; arrange cables beautifully; postcards. `[S · ★★]`
112. **Daily**: a validated, procedurally generated room each day; a spoiler-free share. `[M · ★★]`
113. **Hot-swap charging**: each phone needs 3 s of charge; there are fewer sockets than phones; rotate the charging in time. A calming plate-spinning. `[M · ★★★]`
114. **Co-op async**: a friend's solved room appears as a *ghost layer* in yours. `[L · ★★]`

## H · Meta mechanics (time, mirror, scale, memory)

115. **Ghost cables**: your previous attempt remains as translucent "hot" cables that now block; find a *different* route. Iterative improvement. `[M · ★★★]`
116. **Time scrub**: drag a timeline to rewind the room; in some levels you *need* it to put a plug in the right place at the right moment. `[M · ★★★]`
117. **Past-you duet**: record 8 s of your actions; a ghost hand replays them while you act: a ghost holds the strip while you plug. Two hands through time. `[L · ★★★]`
118. **Mirror room**: a mirrored half-room; moving a plug moves its twin; symmetric solutions. `[M · ★★★]`
119. **Two-hand holds**: multitouch: hold two plugs at once to span a gap. `[M · ★★]`
120. **Memory flash**: socket keys are shown for 2 s then hidden under tape. `[S · ★★]`
121. **Shake-to-reveal**: shake the phone to jiggle hidden things loose (or shuffle). `[S · ★]`
122. **Numbered seating**: plugs carry numbers; strips accept in ascending order. `[S · ★]`
123. **Tuned sockets**: a socket that only accepts a plug *while the speaker plays its note*: sound as a key. `[M · ★★]`

## Mashup matrix (where the garden gets interesting)

| × | Reach | Fat bricks | Switch | Dead socket | No-cross / bridges | Moving socket |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Sliding rail** | Move the strip to *shorten* reach | Slide so the brick overhang fits a gap | Switch rides the rail | Slide to bring the live ones near | Strip position determines crossing | A rail that is itself on a conveyor |
| **Stretchy** | The fling becomes a reach extender | A heavy plug that *fails* to fling | — | Fling over the dead one | Flung cable lands in a bad crossing | Fling at a moving target |
| **Cat** | A cat sits on the near socket; pick another reach | Cat sits on the brick (blocks it) | Cat hits the switch! | Cat prefers the dead socket (it's warm) | Cat bats a cable into a crossing | Cat rides the train |
| **Zero-g** | Drift extends reach; momentum rules | Bricks have inertia (heavy to flick) | Float to the switch | — | Cables swirl across each other | Dock with a moving port |
| **Fan** | Wind straightens slack to gain length | Wind pushes bricks into place | Wind flips a switch | Blow soot off? | Wind blows cables across | Stabilize a swaying socket |
| **Lamp / dark** | See the far socket to know it is reachable | Footprints glow only in light | Switch glows | Dead sockets glow wrong | Cables glow; crossings flare | Moving sockets wink in and out |
| **Mirror** | Symmetric reach; asymmetric obstacles | Mirrored footprints | Two switches, one flip | One dead twin | Mirror makes crossing impossible (a bug-as-feature) | Mirrored trains |
| **Time scrub** | Rewind a too-far drag | Rewind the brick | — | — | Rewind the crossing | **Catch the train:** rewind to re-time |

Fill in any cell you like; most of the 5th-world "Remix Lab" levels should come from this table.

## The wild-card pile

Ideas that did not fit a family but should not be lost:

- A level where the strip is *inside a drawer* and you open the drawer by dragging a handle with your other thumb.
- A "Please Hold" level where a device wants *both* a plug and a hand on it (touch-and-hold) while you plug a second device.
- A level where the **mug is the only thing you cannot pass**, but if you *knock it* (drag a plug into it) it slides one step like a Sokoban block.
- Sounds as puzzle: sockets that glow on the beat; plug in on the beat for a *perfect* chime (optional).
- A level with **twins**: two identical rooms side by side that must both be solved with the *same plug sequence* (you move one room's plugs, the other copies).
- **Devices that sing harmony**: if two awake devices are adjacent, they harmonize (a fifth); the room's adjacency graph is a chord; a stray note is a hint.
- A **fog level**: a mist slowly drifts across the room; plug *before* it covers a socket, then it only matters for memory.
- **Cable art**: unlock a sandbox in which the cables you laid out in a level can be saved as drawings (the cable as a pen).
- A **mirror boss**: your own previous best solve of the same room plays as a ghost opponent, plugging along with you.
- A final-level "Plug the sun": a huge socket across the whole screen and a cable that spans every room you have ever played.
