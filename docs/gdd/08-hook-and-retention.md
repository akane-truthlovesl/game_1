# 08 · Hook & Retention

*Why "one more level"?* Not a trick bolted on at the end, and nothing to do with monetization (out of scope). It is **seven loops running at seven speeds, each feeding the next**, plus a handful of psychological levers used honestly.

---

## 1 · The seven loops

| # | Loop | Scale | The player feels | What feeds it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Touch** | 1 s | The thunk | Feel Bible: weight, snap, sound, haptic |
| 2 | **Wake** | 5 s per device | A device comes alive; a chime climbs | Wake performances, chime ladder, light pools |
| 3 | **Solve** | 15–90 s | "Oh!" | One aha per level; honest traps |
| 4 | **Finish** | 6 s | The whole room blooms | Cascade, chord, stars |
| 5 | **Settle → Swap** | 5–15 s | Affection, curiosity | Poking the room; secrets; the next room's strange new object |
| 6 | **Collect** | minutes–days | "Just one more postcard" | Postcards, cast, skins, secrets, the House Map |
| 7 | **Ritual** | days–weeks | "My morning plug-in" | Daily room, fidget strip, streak that never punishes |

The design rule: **a player who only ever experiences loop 1 should still want to come back.** Everything above it is a reason to stay longer, never a toll.

---

## 2 · The levers (and how we use each one honestly)

### Micro-rewards at every scale
Thunk → chime → light → cascade → stars → postcard → unlock. No gap longer than ~20 seconds without a reward *of some size*. Rewards escalate within a level (each connection is a higher note, a brighter room) and across levels (bigger cascades for bigger rooms).

### The Zeigarnik pull (unfinished tension)
- **Dimness is tension.** A half-lit room *wants* to be lit.
- **The teaser.** The finished room's table slides away to reveal one *strange new object* in the next room before it fully arrives. The player wants to know what that thing does.
- **The last dark device.** One sleeping phone in a room of glowing ones is the most powerful "one more move" image in the game.

### Competence and the aha
The core retention engine is the **feeling of being clever**. Every puzzle level has one insight a thoughtful player can *find*. The validator's luck score (chapter 05) keeps us honest: if random dragging solves it, it is not a puzzle. After a hard level, a breather gives a payoff without demanding thought.

### Autonomy and taste
- Tidy cables are an **aesthetic itch** the game encourages but never demands.
- **Cable skins and chime sets** let players express taste.
- **Photo mode / postcards** let them show it off.

### Variable delight (not variable reward)
No loot boxes, no random drops. Variety comes from **content surprises**: a different device wake-up each time, a secret in every room, the occasional scripted twist (the Power Cut), a mystery box with a funny reveal. The *next* thing is always a little unpredictable; the *rules* never are.

### Flow
The sawtooth difficulty (chapter 09): teach → apply → twist → breathe → boss. The **solve time** rhythm: 15 s, 40 s, 25 s, 60 s, 15 s, 90 s, 45 s, 150 s: never the same pace twice in a row.

### Sensory completeness
The click, the sounds, the haptics, the glow: players keep coming back for the *sensation*. The **fidget strip** lets them have it with no puzzle at all.

### Completion and the House Map
A **cutaway house** is the level map. Each solved room lights its window; each act lights a floor; finishing a world lights a whole building. The completion drive is aimed at *beauty*, not a checklist: you are lighting a little glowing city of your own making.

## 3 · Meta layer: what the player collects

All collectibles are **cosmetic and joyful**; nothing gates the puzzles.

### 3.1 Postcards
Every solved room auto-captures a *postcard*: the lit room, a handwritten caption from the first device to wake, the stars earned, and a small date stamp. The **album** is a page of polaroids, the shareable image of the game. Variants: **Tidy** (a tidy-ribbon), **Clean** (a clean stamp), **Found the secret** (a sticker).

### 3.2 The Cast
Every device type is a character with a card: name, a one-line personality, favorite note, favorite socket (a heart appears if seated there; a tiny secret). New cast members arrive with new worlds and *join the postcards*.

### 3.3 Cable skins and chime sets
- **Skins** (braid, rope, licorice, neon, phone cord, garden hose, ribbon, spaghetti, vine): change *look and sound*.
- **Chime sets:** bell (default), marimba, harp, music box, glass, synth, kalimba.
- Earned from **stars, secrets and worlds**, not bought.

### 3.4 Secrets
One tappable secret per room (a lost sock, a gold button, a ladybird, a tiny key). Finding all of an act's secrets gives that act a **gold border** on the House Map and a unique postcard sticker. Secrets are *discoverable by curiosity in the Settle*, never required.

### 3.5 The Desk Diary
Sticky notes in the corner accumulate a tiny doodle per solved level; by the end of an act it is a comic strip of the day. A tiny, loving, share-worthy artifact.

## 4 · The surprise budget

A calm game needs *regular* violations of its rules. The budget keeps surprise **rhythmic**, **earned**, and **never fatiguing**:

| Rhythm | What | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Every level | A micro-surprise | A new wake-up animation, a decor reaction, a one-word caption |
| Every ~3 levels | A rule wobble | A scripted event (the Power Cut), a new object that behaves unexpectedly |
| Every 5 levels | A set piece | A level with its own look, sound and feel |
| Every 10 levels | A boss | A bigger room, a bigger payoff, a story beat |
| Every ~25 levels | A genre shift | A physics world, a rhythm world, a time-loop world |

**Rules of surprise:** (1) establish the pattern first, then break it; (2) a surprise must be *visible* before it is *understood* (the cat's tail before the cat); (3) the click is never the thing that surprises: it is the one constant.

## 5 · Session design

| Session length | What the player gets |
| --- | --- |
| **30 seconds** | A single *quick-win* level or the fidget strip |
| **3 minutes** | 3–4 levels with a payoff each and a visible next teaser |
| **10 minutes** | A mini-world (5 levels) with its boss, a postcard, a clear stopping point |
| **Daily** | The *Morning Plug*: one small procedural room, a streak, a share |

- **Natural stopping points** (end of each 5-level world) are *celebrated* so players can leave on a high and want to come back.
- **Resume anywhere:** the exact state of every plug is saved continuously.
- **One-more nudges:** the *Next* glow appears with the Wake; the next room's teaser slides in. Quitting is a decision; continuing is not.

## 6 · Stuck handling (kind, never nagging)

| Time without progress | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| ~20 s | The Hint button's bulb gently *breathes* |
| ~45 s | The bulb glows and a tiny ghost finger appears once and fades |
| Tap Hint | A ghost hand shows *one* correct move (never the full solution); infinite taps |
| ~3 min | A second tier: *Show me why* (highlights the blocked socket, the too-short cable) |
| Any time | Undo, Restart, and "Skip with a postcard of shame" (a charming, no-penalty skip that marks the level to revisit) |

## 7 · The daily ritual and the fidget strip

- **Morning Plug:** a small validated daily room (20–45 s). A **streak** counts days but is **never lost**: missing a day quietly spends a "sleep-in" token (earned automatically), so the streak is *protected by kindness*.
- **Share:** a spoiler-free, Wordle-style emoji grid of lit devices (💡 ⬛), stars, time.
- **Fidget strip:** a strip with endless sockets and a bucket of plugs on the home screen: pure sound and haptic. A cozy toy that also *is* the retention of the core sensation.

## 8 · Social without pressure

- **Postcards** are shareable images (no accounts needed).
- **Ghost cables of a friend's best run** (async, opt-in) appear as a translucent layer in a room *you both solved*.
- **No leaderboards by default** (pressure); optional "best tidy" gallery.

## 9 · What we will not do

- No manipulative streak loss, fake scarcity, countdown shops, nag pop-ups, or mid-level interstitials.
- No timers that can *fail* a level.
- No paywalled hints. No hints that run out.
- No dark patterns in the daily system.
- (Monetization is out of scope for this document. If it is ever added, it must live outside the puzzle loop and cannot touch any of the above.)

## 10 · What to measure when there are players

Qualitative first (watch five strangers):
- Time to first plug-in; does it happen without reading? Do they plug a second time for no reason?
- Where do they pause? What do they say aloud? (Speech is the cheapest analytics.)
- Do they ask "what does *that* do?" about the teaser object?
- Do they poke things in the Settle? Do they smile at the cascade?

Quantitative later (anonymous, local-first): time per level, undo count, hint taps, the level they stop at, tidy-star rate, secret-find rate, return-next-day.

## Open water

- **The room remembers you.** The next day, the title desk shows *last night's* arrangement and a note: "Left on."
- **A tiny story arc** told entirely through the desk diary and the postcard captions, with the cat as a recurring character.
- **A cooperative 'plug-it-forward':** every player's finished act lights a window in a shared skyline (anonymous, ambient).
- **A "Bedtime" mode:** a slow, sleepy, sound-only reverse game where you *unplug* everything softly as the house goes to sleep. The retention loop's mirror image.
- **Seasonal micro-worlds** (autumn rain, holiday lights, spring cleaning) that reuse the engine and spark returning players.
